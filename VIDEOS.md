# Videos to Listen To

A listening plan to go with [STUDY_PLAN.md](STUDY_PLAN.md), for times when you have your ears free but not your hands, like at work.

**A caveat before you start:** most algorithm videos are visual, with someone drawing arrows and pointers on a whiteboard. Listening only works best when you **already have a picture in your head**. So the rule of thumb is:

- **Concept courses** (Part 1) listen well *before* you start a category. They build vocabulary and intuition.
- **Problem walkthroughs** (Part 2) listen well *after* you've attempted the problem yourself. You'll recognize what's being drawn, and they won't spoil a problem you haven't tried yet.

If a section clearly needs the screen (a diagram of a tree rotation, a DP table filling in), bookmark the timestamp and watch that part at home.

All links below were checked and resolve to the named channel.

## Part 2 — Problem walkthroughs

**[NeetCode 150 – Review](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLot-Xpze53lfJlNm5S0fq3AmoyugNGqPk)** (NeetCode's official playlist)

This playlist has an explanation for every problem in the study plan. The same videos are also embedded on each problem's page at [neetcode.io/practice](https://neetcode.io/practice). He explains the idea before writing any code, so the first half of each video is the part worth listening to.

**How to use it:**
1. Attempt the problem at home the night before.
2. The next day at work, listen to that problem's video.
3. Compare his approach to yours. Did he use a trick you missed? Was there a faster approach?
4. That evening, spend your 10–15 minute review block re-solving it from a blank file.

**Don't** listen to a video for a problem you haven't tried yet. Hearing the trick before you struggle with it is the fastest way to "understand" a solution you can't reproduce.

**Easy win to start with:** listen to the videos for the 9 problems you've already solved. It's free review, and you'll hear which ideas he stresses.

Related: [Leetcode BLIND-75 Solutions](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLot-Xpze53ldVwtstag2TL4HQhAnC8ATf), NeetCode's older playlist. It overlaps heavily with the 150, so use it if a problem's video in the 150 playlist is missing or you want a second explanation.

## Part 3 — Channels for when you're stuck on a concept

If a topic isn't clicking, look it up on one of these channels. Search inside the channel for the topic name.

| Channel | Best for |
|---|---|
| [NeetCode](https://www.youtube.com/@NeetCode) | Problem explanations, roadmap and career videos |
| [NeetCodeIO](https://www.youtube.com/@NeetCodeIO) | NeetCode's second channel: LeetCode daily problems and longer talks. Good for listening. |
| [Abdul Bari](https://www.youtube.com/@abdul_bari) | Slow, thorough algorithm theory: why an algorithm works, not just how. Good for DP and graphs. |
| [WilliamFiset](https://www.youtube.com/@WilliamFiset-videos) | Graph theory and advanced data structures (union find, Dijkstra, topological sort), with Java code |
| [Back To Back SWE](https://www.youtube.com/@BackToBackSWE) | Long, conversational explanations of classic interview problems. Easy to follow by ear. |
