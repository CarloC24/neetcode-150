# NeetCode 250 Daily Roadmap

A day-by-day plan through every problem on the [NeetCode 250](https://neetcode.io/practice) list that isn't solved yet. The NeetCode 250 contains the whole NeetCode 150, plus 100 extra problems that fill the gaps between them. Most of these are warm-up easies and close variations of a 150 problem.

The problem list comes from [neetcode.io/solutions](https://neetcode.io/solutions) as of November 17, 2026. The links go to each problem's NeetCode solution page.

**Legend:** ★ = also in the NeetCode 150 · E / M / H = Easy / Medium / Hard

## At a glance

|---|---|

| Study days | 203 |

| New-problem time | ~195h total, ~58 min per study day on average |

## How each day works

**Study days (about 75–95 min):**
1. **Review (10–15 min).** Re-solve the problem in the *Review* column from a blank file, without opening your old solution. It's a problem you finished about a week earlier, so you have to recall the pattern instead of just remembering the code. If you get stuck, reread that problem's README and put it back in the queue for next week.
2. **New problems (up to ~80 min).** Solve the day's problems in the order listed. Write the Python and Java versions, then the README, following the steps in [README.md](README.md#adding-a-new-problem).
3. **Wrap up (5 min).** Run `./example.sh <folder>`, then commit. Put a ✅ in the day's *Done* column here, and check the problem off in [STUDY_PLAN.md](STUDY_PLAN.md) if it's ★.

**How the days are packed.** Estimates are the same as in the study plan: ~30 min for an Easy, ~50 for a Medium and ~80 for a Hard, counting both languages. Each day holds at most ~80 minutes of new work, so a day is two Easies, an Easy and a Medium, one Medium, or one Hard. Within a category, problems go Easy → Medium → Hard, and the order otherwise follows the NeetCode list.

**Review days:** re-solve three problems from the past six study days, from a blank file and timed, without notes. Pick the ones you found hardest. Then skim the [PATTERNS.md](PATTERNS.md) sections for those categories and add anything you learned. You can also take the day off.

**Falling behind is fine.** The dates are targets, not deadlines. If a problem runs long, stop at your time limit and finish it tomorrow. Everything after that shifts by a day. Go by the day number, not the date.

**Short on time?** Do only the ★ problems first. That's the NeetCode 150 track, the same one as [STUDY_PLAN.md](STUDY_PLAN.md). Then come back for the rest.
